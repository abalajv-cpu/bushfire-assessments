Bushfire regulation in Queensland works at two stages: planning approval, which looks at whether and how land can be developed, and building approval, which looks at how the building is constructed.

## Planning: the State Planning Policy and council overlays

The State Planning Policy identifies bushfire prone areas across Queensland. Councils reflect that mapping in a bushfire hazard overlay in their planning scheme. Development on land in the overlay is assessed against an overlay code that deals with lot layout, access, water supply and separation from hazardous vegetation.

A site-specific assessment can refine the state mapping. Where the mapped hazard does not match what is on the ground, the report documents the actual vegetation and slope.

## Building: AS 3959

In designated bushfire prone areas, new homes are built to the National Construction Code, which calls up AS 3959. The BAL rating for the site sets the construction requirements.

## What this means for a project

- Check the overlay before you buy or design
- Get bushfire advice at concept stage for subdivisions
- Allow for asset protection zones in the layout
- Confirm access and water supply requirements early
