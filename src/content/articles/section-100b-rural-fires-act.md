Section 100B of the Rural Fires Act 1997 requires certain developments on bush fire prone land in New South Wales to obtain a Bush Fire Safety Authority from the NSW Rural Fire Service before they can be approved.

## Which developments need one

- Subdivision of bush fire prone land that could lawfully be used for residential or rural residential purposes
- Special fire protection purpose developments, including schools, childcare centres, hospitals, aged care, tourist accommodation and group homes

## How the process works

These applications are integrated development. Council refers the application to the RFS, which assesses it against Planning for Bush Fire Protection 2019 and issues general terms of approval if it is satisfied.

The application has to be supported by a bush fire assessment report that addresses asset protection zones, access, water and utilities, construction standards, landscaping and, for special fire protection purposes, emergency management.

## A single dwelling is different

A new house or an extension on an existing lot is normally assessed by council under section 4.14 of the Environmental Planning and Assessment Act and does not need a Bush Fire Safety Authority.
